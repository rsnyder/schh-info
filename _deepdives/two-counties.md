# Fact-check and evidence audit: *One Community, Two Counties*

**Draft audited:** `SCHH_Two_Counties_Article_v4.md`  
**Evidence reviewed:** `SCHH_Beaufort_Jasper_Research_Package (2).md`; `Beaufort vs Jasper County Guide(1).md`; `Sun_City_Hilton_Head_Beaufort_Jasper_Draft(2).md`; `schh-two-counties-draft(1).md`; and the linked primary sources.  
**Evidence cutoff used by the article:** August 21, 2026.

# 1. Overall Assessment

**Needs Moderate Revision.**

The article’s central thesis, principal demographic figures, adopted budget figures, tax caveats, planning descriptions, and most project distinctions are reliable. The problems are concentrated rather than structural, but several matter: countywide growth is sometimes described as growth on one side of Sun City; permit counts are converted into a “nearly built out” conclusion that is not true of East Argent; design procurement is described as active design; Jasper’s referendum allocations are described too narrowly and too close to project funding; and a regional BJWSA plan is treated as proof that the specific Hardeeville pipeline has been incorporated. Several citations also support only part of the sentence they follow. These are repairable with surgical edits, and the corrected article below preserves the draft’s voice and structure.

# 3. Important Qualifications Lost in Drafting

1. **Countywide data are not side-of-community data.** Census figures describe Beaufort and Jasper Counties as wholes; they do not quantify growth, age, density, or household characteristics within the two portions of Sun City.
2. **Entitled, master-planned, permitted, built, and occupied are different stages.** The draft preserves that distinction at the citywide level but weakens it when calling both Sun City tracts “nearly built out.” East Argent is only about half permitted, and neither permit count is an occupancy count.
3. **Design procurement is not active design.** The public index verifies a solicitation, but the package did not locate an award, contract, notice to proceed, design percentage, state-plan approval, construction bid, or activation date.
4. **Referendum allocations are not project status.** Jasper’s voter-approved program assigns estimated amounts to transportation and greenbelt purposes; it does not establish that every named project is fully funded, designed, or under construction.
5. **Regional forecasting is not proof that a specific ledger was modeled.** BJWSA’s 20-year regional planning supports the regional frame but not the stronger claim that Hardeeville’s June 2026 pipeline is already “factored in.”
6. **Failure to locate a record is not proof that no record exists.** This applies to the Jasper greenbelt acquisition sentence and, as the draft’s Editorial Notes correctly recognize, to the absence of a visible Argent/Sundance design award.
7. **Service eligibility remains address- and provider-specific.** City status, fire district, trash arrangement, tax district, and some utility/service details should be confirmed from current records rather than inferred from “Sun City,” county, or ZIP code.
8. **The signal window is analysis, not a project schedule.** “First half of 2027” is defensible only as the author’s moderate-confidence planning assumption, with the no-official-date warning adjacent to it.

# 5. Interpretations Worth Reviewing

These passages are defensible editorial framing rather than conclusions directly stated by a source. They need not be removed, but readers should be able to recognize their status.

1. **“A mature county beside a growth frontier” / “larger, older, more built-out.”** This fairly synthesizes population, age, density, planning history, and development data, but “mature” and “built-out” are the author’s characterization.
2. **Hardeeville as “a city building itself out.”** The capital budget and pipeline make the interpretation plausible; neither source uses that conclusion as a measured finding.
3. **“Growth pressure on the community’s western side is real.”** This reasonably follows from Hardeeville’s documented pipeline, but the record does not quantify the resulting pressure specifically within the Jasper-side Sun City neighborhoods.
4. **“The governments know what residents experience daily.”** The cross-county SC 170 coordination proposal supports the thought, but the sentence attributes awareness and motive.
5. **“Anything less is folklore.”** The evidence strongly supports the narrower conclusion that partial-millage comparisons are incomplete. “Folklore” is rhetorical judgment.
6. **The first half of 2027 as the “more credible” signal window.** This is the most consequential interpretation. It is a scenario based on known procedural steps and a prior signal, not a Hardeeville or SCDOT schedule.
7. **“Residents still have a direct say.”** Accurate in the electoral sense, but editorial in tone and selective because public participation can occur at other stages too.
8. **“Shared systems, shared exposure.”** This is an organizing frame synthesized from BJWSA, evacuation, hospital, and environmental evidence.
9. **Conservation as “a difference in maturity, not in values.”** This is a responsible corrective to an unsupported values comparison, but it remains an interpretation; the sources establish program age, acreage, and allocation, not civic values.

# One Community, Two Counties

## A resident's guide to the line you can't see, and what it means for your taxes, roads, and services

*Facts current as of August 21, 2026. Tax rates, budgets, and project schedules change; dates are noted where it matters.*

---

Two neighbors in Sun City Hilton Head can share a golf course, a pickleball schedule, and a favorite table at the same restaurant, and still live under different county governments. One gets a tax bill from Beaufort County. The other hears from Jasper County, possibly with a City of Hardeeville line on the bill as well. They may vote in different elections and use different library systems. Inside the gates, nothing marks the boundary. Outside them, it matters.

That invisible line explains a lot about what is happening around the community right now: why Jasper County is growing much faster than Beaufort County, why "which side is cheaper" is harder to answer than the folklore suggests, and why some road projects are under construction while others are still just ideas in a plan.

**The short version:**

- The community name alone does not establish jurisdiction. Your address-specific official records identify your county, taxes, voting districts, and services.
- From the 2020 estimates base to July 2025, Jasper County’s estimated growth rate was more than three and a half times Beaufort’s, but Beaufort remained more than five times as populous.
- Nobody has proven which side is cheaper to live on. The common shorthand comparisons omit important parts of the tax calculation.
- Big road money is in motion: Jasper's program is approved, Beaufort's goes to voters this November, I-95 work is underway, and the new light at the Sun City West entrance is approved and was in design procurement (no official switch-on date yet).
- Many of the systems that matter most, including water, hospitals, hurricane planning, and emergency response, already operate across the county line.

The rest of this guide fills in the details, and ends with a checklist for answering the questions that depend on your own address.

## Start with the address, not the label

"Sun City" is a community name, not a jurisdiction. A mailing address or ZIP code by itself does not tell you which county a home is in, whether it sits inside Hardeeville's city limits, or which tax districts apply.

What the records do show: the community spans both counties, and Hardeeville's zoning library lists planned development districts for the Argent 2 and East Argent Sun City tracts, meaning the city has a formal planning role over those Jasper-side sections ([Hardeeville zoning](https://hardeevillesc.gov/2178/Zoning)). A planned development district is a custom set of zoning rules negotiated for a large project.

What the records do not show is the exact profile of any particular home. For that, three sources are authoritative: your tax bill, your parcel record, and your voter registration at [MySCVotes](https://scvotes.gov/). When in doubt, trust those over neighborhood shorthand.

## A mature county beside a growth frontier

The two counties are on very different paths, and a few numbers tell the story.

| | Beaufort County | Jasper County |
|---|---:|---:|
| Population (July 2025 estimate) | 204,433 | 38,533 |
| Growth, 2020 to 2025 (estimate) | 9.3% | 33.7% |
| Residents 65 and older | 31.2% | 28.0% |
| People per square mile (2020) | 325 | 44 |

Source: [Census QuickFacts](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/beaufortcountysouthcarolina/PST045225) for [both counties](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jaspercountysouthcarolina/PST045225). These are countywide figures, not a profile of Sun City households or a measure of growth on each side of the community.

In plain terms: Beaufort is the larger, older, more built-out county. Jasper is much smaller and mostly rural, but it is adding people at a remarkable rate. Hardeeville is a major focus of the documented development pipeline.

How much development is in that pipeline? Hardeeville's own ledger is the best public measure of the citywide total ([Residential Pipeline, June 2026](https://hardeevillesc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/19438/Residential-Pipeline-June-2026-PDF)):

| Hardeeville pipeline, June 2026 | Units |
|---|---:|
| Maximum allowed under development agreements | 49,258 |
| In master plans | 20,103 |
| Building permits issued | 9,000 |

Those three numbers describe three different stages, and the difference matters. The 49,258 figure is the maximum currently allowed under existing agreements, not a forecast. Master plans are closer to reality, and permits closer still, but permits are not proof of construction or occupancy. Within the pipeline, Argent 2 is nearly fully permitted, with 1,480 planned units and 1,396 permits issued; East Argent shows 1,540 planned units and 791 permits, leaving substantial unpermitted capacity.

That does not mean fifty thousand homes are on the way. But the growth pressure around the community's western side is real, documented, and trackable through public records.

## Who is planning what

Three governments hold the pens around the community, each with its own playbook.

**Beaufort County** works from Envision Beaufort County 2040, adopted in 2021 and now getting its five-year update ([the plan](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/council/comprehensive-plan/2040_CompPlan_Adopted.pdf)). A comprehensive plan sets long-term priorities without committing money. The companion [Action Playbook](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/council/comprehensive-plan/2040_ActionPlaybook_Adopted.pdf) goes further, proposing a new Okatie-area library, expanded EMS capacity near Sun City at William Pope Drive, and formal three-way coordination with Jasper County and Hardeeville on the SC 170 corridor. These are proposals, not funded construction.

**Jasper County** is updating its 2018 plan, Jasper's Journey, and has proposed an overlay district to manage growth in the rural Euhaw area; that proposal had not been confirmed as final law at press time ([county planning pages](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/services/planning-and-building/comprehensive-master-plan/)).

**Hardeeville** governs the fastest-changing ground through its development districts and a budget that reflects a city building itself out: $93.3 million total for FY2027, including $29.4 million for capital projects ([budget release](https://hardeevillesc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/19313/2027-Hardeeville-Budget-Release-)).

The detail worth noticing: Beaufort's playbook explicitly proposes working across the county line on SC 170. The governments know what residents experience daily. Traffic and drainage do not stop at the county sign.

## Read the bill, not the myth

Ask around and you will hear confident claims about which side is "cheaper." The record supports something less satisfying and more useful: nobody has actually established an answer, and the usual comparisons get the mechanics wrong.

South Carolina property tax works in layers. An owner-occupied primary home (a "legal residence" in tax language) is assessed at 4 percent of market value; most other real property is assessed at 6 percent ([SCDOR assessment ratios](https://dor.sc.gov/local-government-services)). A mill is one dollar of tax per thousand dollars of assessed value, so on a $100,000 legal residence, ten mills equals $40 before credits. Qualifying legal residences are exempt from school *operating* taxes, though school debt still applies ([Beaufort County tax calculator](https://www.beaufortcountyauditor.com/calc)). And residents 65 and older who qualify get the state Homestead Exemption, which removes the first $50,000 of market value from taxation ([SCDOR Homestead guidance](https://dor.sc.gov/property/exempt-property)).

Here are the county-adopted levies, with a large caution: this is **not a complete tax bill** for either side.

| Adopted levy | Mills |
|---|---:|
| Beaufort County operating, FY2027 | 59.5 |
| Jasper County operating, FY2026-27 | 115.0 |
| Jasper County debt | 5.0 |
| Jasper school operating (legal residences exempt) | 154.0 |
| Jasper school debt | 25.0 |
| Jasper Rural Fire, where it applies | 28.9 |

Sources: [Beaufort FY2027 budget](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/news/2026/06/beaufort-county-council-passes-fy2027-budget.html); [Jasper adopted budgets](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/government/financial-administrative-services/).

Why can't you just compare those numbers? Three reasons. The Beaufort figure is the county government's portion only; Beaufort homes also pay school, fire, and other district levies not shown here. A Jasper-side home inside Hardeeville may add a municipal layer (the city's final FY2027 rate was not yet verified). And Jasper's local-option sales tax generates a credit that lowers property-tax bills, which millage tables ignore entirely.

The honest bottom line: comparing the two sides requires two actual tax bills, for comparable homes, in the same year, with all exemptions and credits applied. Anything less is folklore.

Sales tax is simpler. The general rate is 6 percent in Beaufort County and 9 percent in Jasper ([SCDOR rate table](https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/forms/ST500.pdf)), though unprepared food is exempt from two of Jasper's three extra pennies, so the grocery gap is smaller than the headline suggests ([Jasper County tax FAQ](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/services/taxes/property-assessments/assessor-appeals/frequently-asked-questions/)).

## The roads: what's real, what's funded, what's just a plan

Road projects climb a ladder: long-range plan, ballot proposal, funded program, design, construction. Each rung is real news, but only the top ones move traffic. Here is where things stand, starting with the item closest to home.

**Approved and in design procurement: the new light at the Sun City West entrance.** The long-discussed traffic signal at Sundance Drive and Argent Boulevard is a documented City of Hardeeville project, and it is moving. SCDOT gave the city written approval on February 13, 2026 to proceed, after traffic studies confirmed the intersection qualifies for a signal under the state's criteria ([city announcement](https://hardeevillesc.gov/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=1323)). The city's detailed proposed FY2027 capital plan carries it as a $550,000 project, $50,000 for design and $500,000 for construction, funded by developer agreement fees, the payments builders make to the city under their development deals ([FY2027 proposed budget book, pp. 150-151](https://hardeevillesc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/19186/FY27-Proposed-Budget-to-Council-)). And by April 28, 2026, the city had advertised for engineering-design services ([procurement listing](https://www.southcarolinabids.com/bid_opportunities/2026/04/28/)), so the project had reached design procurement. The reviewed sources did not establish a design award or design progress. It is a separate project from the US 278/Sun City signal on Beaufort County's November ballot; that one is a different intersection, a different government, and still awaiting a vote.

So when does it light up? The honest answer is that no official date was found in the sources reviewed. A November 2026 estimate has circulated among residents, but no published schedule or project milestone supports it; hitting that date would require the design award, state plan review, permits, construction bidding, and equipment delivery all to go quickly. As an analytical—not official—planning assumption, the first half of 2027 is more credible, and later remains possible if reviews, bids, or hardware run long. The city's previous signal, on Latitude Boulevard, offers useful perspective on why these projects take time: it ran about a year from engineering authorization to construction, with the city attributing much of the wait to a 30-to-40-week manufacturing lead time for the mast arms that hold the signal heads ([Hardeeville Latitude Boulevard update](https://hardeevillesc.gov/CivicAlerts.asp?AID=700)). Different project, possibly different equipment, but the same multi-step process. Every date beyond the completed steps is an estimate, not a commitment; the next hard news to watch for is a design award, and after that a construction contract.

**Under construction now.** SCDOT is rebuilding I-95 in its mile-marker 0–8 project, including bridge work at the Savannah River and SC 46. John Smith Road is closed for bridge replacement through late 2026. The next stretch, mile markers 8 to 21, is in design, with construction expected in 2027 ([mile markers 0–8](https://www.fixthedrive95.com/mm0to8); [mile markers 8–21](https://www.fixthedrive95.com/mm8to21)).

**Approved program.** Jasper County voters passed a 1 percent transportation sales tax in November 2024, running up to 15 years and estimated at $470 million: $376 million for transportation and $94 million for greenbelt purposes ([post-election county packet](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/media/ladh2eb2/05302025-tstac-agenda-packet.pdf)). Program documents allocate $57 million for Argent Boulevard and $65 million for two stretches of US 278 between the county line and I-95 ([county referendum materials](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/media/1tula2oj/07242024-agenda-e-packet.pdf)). Allocated, yes; but each project still has to move through design, procurement, and construction individually.

**On the ballot.** Beaufort County voters decide on November 3, 2026 whether to approve an eight-year transportation sales tax estimated at $780 million: $326 million for safety and intersections, $205 million for widening, and $249 million for resurfacing and dirt-road paving. The project list includes SC 170, SC 46, US 278, and a traffic signal project at US 278 and Sun City ([official referendum page](https://beaufortcountypenny.com/proposed-2026-transportation-referendum-2026/)). Nothing is funded until the vote passes, which also makes this the one item on this list where residents still have a direct say.

**On the long-range map.** The regional transportation planning body for the two counties, called LATS, maintains 20-year plans that set priorities and eligibility ([LATS](https://www.lowcountrycog.org/planning_and_transportation/2045_lats_lrtp/index.php)). Useful, but a long-range plan is not a construction schedule.

## Emergencies: the nearest truck, not the nearest boundary

Emergency response is one place where governments have deliberately worked around the line. On the Beaufort side, Bluffton Township Fire District's Station 34 on William Pope Drive houses both a fire engine and Beaufort County EMS ([Station 34](https://blufftonfd.com/station/station-34-sun-city/)), and the district broke ground in May 2026 on Station 39 at 301 Sun City Boulevard, its tenth station ([Station 39 groundbreaking](https://blufftonfd.com/bluffton-township-fire-district-breaks-ground-on-station-39-its-10th-fire-station/)).

In southern Jasper County, Hardeeville's Operation Phoenix coordinates dispatch so the closest available engine or ambulance responds whenever possible, regardless of whose name is on the truck ([Operation Phoenix](https://hardeevillesc.gov/2802/Operation-Phoenix)).

Two things to keep straight. In an emergency, call 911; the coordination happens behind the scenes. And closest-unit response does not change which fire district taxes your property, so your nonemergency contacts are still worth confirming by address.

## Shared systems, shared exposure

Some of the most important services never respected the county line in the first place.

**Water and sewer** come from the Beaufort-Jasper Water & Sewer Authority, a two-county utility whose history includes the original Del Webb Sun City service agreement ([BJWSA Board Handbook](https://bjwsa.org/DocumentCenter/View/486/BJWSA-Board-Handbook)). Its current master plan, adopted in May 2026, uses 20-year regional projections to guide investment ([BJWSA Master Plan](https://bjwsa.org/299/Master-Plan)).

**Hurricanes** are planned regionally too. The state's Know Your Zone system covers both counties, and the state hurricane plan models Beaufort, Jasper, and Colleton as one Southern Hurricane Region ([Know Your Zone](https://www.scemd.org/prepare/know-your-zone/); [2026 Hurricane Plan, Annex C](https://www.scemd.org/media/2202/2026-annex-c-evacuation-zones-and-clearance-times.pdf)). One point deserves emphasis: being on the Jasper side, farther from the beach, does not make a home safe from flooding. Flood risk, insurance needs, and evacuation status must be checked for the individual property and address, not inferred from the county.

**Hospitals** crossed the line in 2024, when Novant Health bought Hilton Head Hospital and Coastal Carolina Hospital in Hardeeville as part of a $2.4 billion purchase ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/releases-20240201)). Novant has also won state approval for a new 50-bed hospital in Bluffton, expandable to 100 beds ([approval announcement](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/s-c-approves-novant-healths-certificate-of-need-application-for-new-50-bed-hospital-in-bluffton)), which Novant currently schedules for 2028 ([2026 update](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/strong-financial-stewardship-drives-high-quality-care-expanded-access-team-development-and-long-term-sustainability-at-novant-health)). Shared ownership makes health care a regional story, though it does not by itself prove anything about quality of care, and 2028 is a schedule, not a promise.

**Conservation** shows the two counties at different stages of the same idea. Beaufort's decades-old land preservation program reports more than 25,000 acres protected ([county update](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/news/2025/12/nine-acre-addition-completes-widgeon-point-preserve%2C-rural-and-critical-land-preservation-program.html)). Jasper's greenbelt program is brand new, created by the 2024 referendum with an estimated $94 million allocated for greenbelt purposes; the sources reviewed for this article did not establish completed acquisitions by the August 21 cutoff. That is a difference in maturity, not in values.

## The everyday details where the line reappears

A few practical services split cleanly by side:

| | Beaufort side | Jasper side |
|---|---|---|
| **Trash** | County convenience centers, subject to [eligibility rules](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/solid-waste-and-recycle/convenience-centers/index.html), or [private curbside haulers](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/solid-waste-and-recycle/convenience-centers/residential-curbside.html) | Hardeeville residents may [enroll with Capital Waste Services](https://hardeevillesc.gov/2528/Trash-Pick-Up); Jasper County operates [six drop-off centers](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/services/public-works/garbage-and-recycling/) for county citizens; confirm any neighborhood or association arrangement |
| **Library** | Beaufort County Library (Bluffton Branch, 120 Palmetto Way) | Allendale-Hampton-Jasper regional system (Hardeeville branch, 30 Main Street) |
| **County council** | [11 members, single-member districts](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/council/index.html) | [5 members: 4 township seats plus 1 at-large](https://www.jaspercountysc.gov/government/council/) |

Library sources: [Bluffton Branch](https://www.beaufortcountylibrary.org/visit/locations/bluffton-branch-library); [Hardeeville Library](https://ahjlibrary.org/hardeeville-library/).

Two open questions worth knowing about: whether a Community Association contract standardizes trash collection for particular neighborhoods, and whether one side's library card works at the other side's library. Neither is settled in the public record, so check directly before assuming. Hardeeville residents also vote in city elections on top of county ones.

## Your address checklist

The most accurate answer to "which side is better?" is a set of better questions, each with an official source:

1. **County and parcel:** your county assessor's parcel record.
2. **City status:** Hardeeville's [current city-limits map](https://hardeevillesc.gov/2265/City-Documents-Maps) for Jasper-side homes.
3. **Tax bill and districts:** your current tax notice, including any city, fire, or special-district lines and the Jasper local-option credit.
4. **Legal residence and Homestead status:** your county auditor, using state eligibility rules.
5. **Voting:** [MySCVotes](https://scvotes.gov/) for your districts, precinct, and polling place.
6. **Fire, EMS, police:** 911 in any emergency; confirm nonemergency contacts by address.
7. **Trash:** your city, county, or association arrangement.
8. **Evacuation zone and flood risk:** [Know Your Zone](https://www.scemd.org/prepare/know-your-zone/) and current flood maps for your property.
9. **Water and sewer:** your BJWSA account.

The county line will not become more visible any time soon. But residents who know which record answers which question can navigate both sides of it with confidence.

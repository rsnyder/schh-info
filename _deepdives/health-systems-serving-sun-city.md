## Four systems, six hospitals, two states — and what "which system" changes for the person in the waiting room

*Facts current as of August 25, 2026. Most sources were retrieved on August 24, 2026; pages re-fetched on August 25, 2026 carry that date where it matters. Quality ratings, construction schedules, and financial filings change; the dataset date or retrieval date is given wherever it matters. This guide reports what each source publishes. It does not rank the systems.*

---

Most residents pick a doctor, not a health system. The system arrives anyway — in the name at the top of the bill, in which hospital your doctor can admit you to, in which website holds your test results, and in where the ambulance takes you when the decision is not yours to make.

Within roughly forty-five minutes of Sun City there are four of them. Two are South Carolina hospital operators; two are in Savannah. Between them they run six acute-care hospitals, two freestanding emergency departments already open in Bluffton, and two more hospitals approved for Bluffton — Beaufort Memorial's already under construction, Novant's still in permitting.

This guide profiles each one using the same set of facts, drawn from official records where they exist and clearly labeled where they do not. It ends with a side-by-side table, questions worth asking your own practice, and an honest account of what this research could not pin down.

## Why this matters

**The admitting hospital.** If your primary-care practice is owned by a system that runs more than one hospital, a scheduled admission usually goes to one of them. None of the four systems publishes which one for its clinics near Sun City. That is the largest gap in this research, and the first question in the list at the end.

**Where the emergency department is.** Two of Bluffton's emergency rooms are *freestanding* — a full 24/7 emergency department in its own building, with no inpatient beds upstairs. If you need admission from one, you are transported to the parent hospital. Which hospital that is, and whose license the ED operates under, determines both the transfer and the bill.

**The portal.** Your records live in whichever electronic system your practice uses. Two of the four describe their portal as MyChart, which their own pages associate with Epic; two use MEDITECH; Beaufort Memorial has two separate logins, one for the hospital and one for its physician practices; and Memorial Health's site names two portal brands, MyChart and MyHealthONE. None of the four systems' portal pages retrieved for this guide names another system with which it shares records — so do not assume a Savannah specialist can see your Bluffton imaging.

**Facility fees.** When a clinic is legally part of a hospital rather than a freestanding doctor's office — the arrangement Medicare calls *provider-based* — one visit can produce two charges: the physician's fee and a hospital "facility fee." No South Carolina facility-fee notice was located for any of the four systems' local sites in this research. That is not proof one never applies; it means nothing was published where a resident would look.

**Medicare Advantage networks.** A Medicare Advantage plan contracts with specific hospitals and specific billing entities. This guide did not research plan networks — they change, and they differ by plan. Check your plan's own directory, and note the date you checked.

## Who is in scope, and why

The rule used here: a system is in scope if it operates an acute-care hospital or hospital-based emergency department within roughly 45 minutes' drive of Sun City (Okatie, SC 29909), or holds an approved Certificate of Need for one. A **Certificate of Need (CON)** is the state permission a hospital must obtain before it can build.

Every Medicare-certified hospital in the surrounding South Carolina and Georgia counties was enumerated from CMS's Hospital General Information dataset and then tested against that rule ([CMS Hospital General Information, released August 13, 2026](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)). Four systems qualified: **Novant Health**, **Beaufort Memorial**, **HCA Healthcare's Memorial Health**, and **St. Joseph's/Candler**.

Excluded: **Colleton Medical Center** (Walterboro) and **Hampton Regional Medical Center** (Varnville), on road distance — no drive time was computed for either; **Naval Hospital Beaufort**, which CMS records show with no emergency services; and Effingham, East Georgia Regional, Liberty Regional, Optim Screven, Winn Army, Georgia Regional Savannah and Coastal Harbor, as outside the rule or not general acute-care hospitals ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)). **Tidelands Health** operates in the Georgetown and Horry County area ([Tidelands](https://www.tidelandshealth.org/locations/hospitals/)).

**MUSC Health** is present locally but not as a hospital operator: it has an affiliation with Beaufort Memorial ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/beaufort-memorial-announces-affiliation-agreement-with-musc-health)) and opened the MUSC Health Bluffton Medical Pavilion at 700 Buckwalter Towne Blvd in June 2026 — a medical office building with no emergency department or inpatient beds ([MUSC Health](https://www.getcare.muschealth.org/locations/sc/bluffton/musc-health-bluffton-medical-paviliondep-fac978)).

**About the drive times.** One set was computed by geocoding the Sun City ZIP-code center and each hospital's published address, then routing between them at free-flow speeds with no traffic, on August 24, 2026, using OpenStreetMap's Nominatim geocoder and the OSRM routing service ([Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search)). A second set came from city-to-city lookups, which measure between town centers rather than addresses. Where they disagree, both are given. **Every drive time here is an estimate**, computed without traffic; the US-278 and Bluffton Parkway corridors are seasonally congested, and a real trip in March is not a free-flow trip.

## Novant Health

Novant Health, Inc. is headquartered at 2085 Frontis Plaza Blvd, Winston-Salem, North Carolina ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/about/company/)), and is a nonprofit — a 501(c)(3) organization, EIN 56-1376950, per IRS filings ([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/561376950)). It bought Hilton Head Hospital, Coastal Carolina Hospital and East Cooper Medical Center from Tenet Healthcare in a transaction Novant announced as closed on February 1, 2024, for approximately $2.4 billion ([Novant, February 1, 2024](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/releases-20240201); [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2024/02/02/coastal-carolina-hilton-head-hospitals-purchased-by-novant-health/)).

The hospital on Hilton Head bills as **Hilton Head Medical Center, LLC**, NPI 1902675101, with a Winston-Salem mailing address and additional practice locations in Bluffton ([CMS NPPES](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/api/?version=2.1&organization_name=HILTON%20HEAD%20MEDICAL%20CENTER*&state=SC)). The names *Hilton Head Medical Center, LLC* and *South Carolina Coastal Medical Center, LLC*, with EINs 93-4604680 and 93-4580630, also appear in the filenames of Novant's own published standard-charge files ([Novant price transparency](https://www.novanthealth.org/for-patients/billing--insurance/price-transparency/)). Neither state corporate registry was checked.

**Hospitals.** *Novant Health Hilton Head Medical Center*, 25 Hospital Center Blvd, Hilton Head Island (Medicare CCN 420080), and *Novant Health Coastal Carolina Medical Center*, 1000 Medical Center Dr, Hardeeville (CCN 420101), which CMS still lists under its former name, "Coastal Carolina Hospital" ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)). Novant states Hilton Head has 93 beds ([Novant facility information](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/facility-information)); the hospital's own location page states no bed count ([Novant location page](https://www.novanthealth.org/locations/medical-centers/hilton-head-medical-center/)). Coastal Carolina's count diverges: Novant's facility page says 44, third-party directories report 37 to 41. No state license was obtained for either, so no bed count in this guide is license-verified. Coastal Carolina is the closest hospital to Sun City — about 10 to 20 minutes, by estimates that range from 9 minutes free-flow to 15–20 minutes city-to-city; Hilton Head Medical Center is roughly 20 to 40 minutes.

**Emergency departments.** Both hospitals have one. Novant also runs **two freestanding emergency departments in Bluffton**. The *Tidewatch Emergency Department*, 349 Tidewatch Dr W, appears on Novant's own location pages ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/pf/location-group?locationID=4681350287794)), which do not state which hospital's license it operates under; Novant's February 2024 acquisition release lists a "Tidewatch Free-Standing Emergency Department" in Bluffton among the assets it bought from Tenet ([Novant, February 1, 2024](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/releases-20240201)). The *Bluffton Emergency Department* at 8 Parkside Dr **opened July 21, 2026** — Novant's own announcement says it is open and seeing patients, 24 hours a day, with advanced diagnostics; one research reading of that announcement reported that it operates under Hilton Head Medical Center's license, but that statement was not found on the page when rechecked and is treated here as not verified ([Novant, July 21, 2026](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/novant-health-expands-bluffton-footprint-with-opening-of-full-service-freestanding-emergency-department); [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2026/07/21/novant-health-opens-second-free-standing-er-bluffton/); [Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/beaufort-county/news/novant-health-bluffton-emergency-department/article_63db58ac-2411-47b2-a556-b6b4e2473ea9.html)). Novant's opening announcement describes a 10,000-square-foot facility with ten treatment rooms, including a trauma room ([Novant, July 21, 2026](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/novant-health-expands-bluffton-footprint-with-opening-of-full-service-freestanding-emergency-department)); press accounts give the same dimensions and add a 40,000-square-foot medical office building beside it ([Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/beaufort-county/news/novant-health-bluffton-emergency-department/article_63db58ac-2411-47b2-a556-b6b4e2473ea9.html)). Neither freestanding ED's drive time was computed with a routing tool.

**Nearby and planned.** Novant Health Okatie Primary Care sits at 40 Okatie Center Blvd South, Suite 100, Okatie 29909, with primary care on Arley Way, general surgery at Belfair, Okatie Surgical Partners and infectious-disease care in Bluffton ([Novant clinic pages](https://www.novanthealth.org/locations/clinics/bluffton-okatie-primary-care/)); no existing Novant urgent-care site near Sun City was verified. *Novant Health Bluffton Medical Center* — 50 beds, expandable to 100, roughly $320 million — received its Certificate of Need on March 24, 2025, with anticipated opening in 2028 ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/s-c-approves-novant-healths-certificate-of-need-application-for-new-50-bed-hospital-in-bluffton); [Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/beaufort-county/news/novant-beaufort-memorial-hospital-bluffton/article_aa394a42-5f5f-41e7-9480-2baf38187e29.html)). Novant places it on a campus it calls Washington Square Medical Park, less than half a mile from the Parkside Drive ED ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/novant-health-breaks-ground-on-bluffton-emergency-department-and-medical-office-building)). South Carolina's Department of Public Health published a Certificate of Need public-hearing notice for the project — hearing set for January 14, 2025 — describing a 50-bed hospital of **196,500 square feet** at a project cost of **$320,049,946.79**, with **33 beds transferred** from existing capacity and **17 new beds** ([SC DPH Certificate of Need public hearing notice](https://dph.sc.gov/events/certificate-need-con-public-hearing-tuesday-january-14-2025)). Which existing hospital the 33 transferred beds come from is not stated in that notice. The signed state CON decision document was not retrieved.

**What membership means.** The portal is MyNovant, which Novant's portal page describes as Epic MyChart ([MyNovant](https://www.novanthealth.org/mynovant/)); whether both South Carolina hospitals are fully on it was not separately verified. Novant's financial-assistance page distinguishes provider-based clinics from non-provider-based ones in North Carolina, ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/for-patients/billing--insurance/financial-assistance/)), and its price page says posted charges are hospital charges only and exclude physician fees ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/for-patients/billing--insurance/price-transparency/)) — but **no South Carolina facility-fee notice was located**. Novant publishes a separate South Carolina financial-assistance policy, and it was read for this guide: it provides free emergency or medically necessary care to eligible patients with annual family income **at or below 300% of the current federal poverty guidelines**, subject to service-area, insurance-coverage, asset and other conditions, and it excludes elective services and services from independent clinicians not covered by the policy. The document identifies December 2024 as its initial effective date, states that the electronic version is the current approved version, and lists a next review date of **December 2027** ([policy PDF](https://www.novanthealth.org/globalassets/buttons-and-documents-ctaslinks/documents-pdfs/policy-sc-financial-assistance.pdf)). That South Carolina policy is a separate document from the widely reported expansion of free care to 300% of the federal poverty level, effective January 2025, which applies to **North Carolina** facilities per Novant's own release ([Novant, December 2024](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/novant-health-bolsters-financial-assistance-policy-for-hospitals-plans-to-relieve-medical-debt-for-thousands)). Machine-readable price files are posted for both South Carolina hospitals; that page carried an April 24, 2026 date ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/for-patients/billing--insurance/price-transparency/)).

**Organization facts.** Novant's most recently extracted Form 990, for fiscal year 2024 as displayed by ProPublica on recheck, reports $6.54 billion in total revenue, $6.19 billion in expenses, $14.56 billion in total assets and $863.3 million in net assets, with reported compensation of $6,943,831 plus $1,207,106 in other compensation for chief executive Carl Armato ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/561376950)). The prior-year (tax year 2023) extract read earlier in this research showed $4.02 billion in revenue and $3.72 billion in net assets; the year-over-year change in the consolidation basis was not examined, and the return covers a single legal entity that may not represent the consolidated system. Novant separately reports $2.1 billion in community benefit for 2025 across 21 medical centers, 730-plus clinics and 43,000-plus team members as of December 31, 2025 ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/about/company/)), and its facility-information page lists the medical centers individually, including the three South Carolina hospitals ([Novant](https://www.novanthealth.org/newsroom/facility-information)). A search of HHS Office for Civil Rights enforcement listings returned no Novant matter ([HHS OCR](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/index.html)); no official enforcement record was located in this research, which is not the same as a clean record.

## Beaufort Memorial

The legal name is **Beaufort County Memorial Hospital**; it operates as Beaufort Memorial, at 955 Ribaut Rd, Beaufort (CCN 420067) ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)). It is the one system here that is neither a private nonprofit nor a public company, and four facts have to travel together: CMS classifies it as **Government – Local** ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)); its trustees are appointed by Beaufort County Council, whose own website calls the hospital board "an agency of Council" ([Beaufort County](https://www.beaufortcountysc.gov/beaufort-memorial-hospital-board/index.html)); it describes itself as not-for-profit and dates itself to 1944 ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/about-us)); and it files no Form 990, as a governmental entity is not required to ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/576000094)). Any single label is incomplete.

**Hospital.** One hospital, licensed for 201 beds — 169 acute, 14 rehabilitation, 18 mental health — as stated by Beaufort Memorial ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/about-us)). Estimated drive time from Sun City is roughly 30 to 45 minutes; the free-flow route is 33 minutes and 24 miles.

**Emergency care.** The hospital emergency department in Beaufort today. Under construction: **Beaufort Memorial Emergency & Urgent Care**, 1016 William Hilton Pkwy on Hilton Head Island, roughly 11,400 square feet, developed with Intuitive Health and MUSC Health ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/work-begins-emergency-urgent-care-hilton-head-island); [WSAV](https://www.wsav.com/news/local-news/beaufort-memorial-partners-with-intuitive-musc-health-for-hilton-head-emergency-urgent-care-facility/)). The entity was enumerated in the federal provider registry at that address on July 29, 2026 ([CMS NPPES](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/api/?version=2.1&organization_name=BEAUFORT%20COUNTY%20MEMORIAL%20HOSPITAL&state=SC)). Completion dates diverge: Beaufort Memorial says fall 2026 ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/work-begins-emergency-urgent-care-hilton-head-island)), the Post and Courier reported early 2027 ([Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/beaufort-county/news/novant-health-bluffton-emergency-department/article_63db58ac-2411-47b2-a556-b6b4e2473ea9.html)).

**Nearby.** The **Okatie Medical Pavilion**, 122 Okatie Center Blvd North, Okatie 29909 — primary care, specialties, imaging, laboratory, and an Express Care urgent-care suite — is inside Sun City's own ZIP code ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/location/okatie-medical-pavilion)), and the federal registry lists organization NPIs at that address ([NPPES](https://npiregistry.cms.hhs.gov/api/?version=2.1&organization_name=BEAUFORT%20COUNTY%20MEMORIAL%20HOSPITAL&state=SC)). System-wide, Beaufort Memorial reports 32 practices, two cancer centers and three Express Care locations ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/about-us)).

**Planned.** *Bluffton Community Hospital* — 28 inpatient beds, roughly $103 million, CON approved March 24, 2025, with a full-service emergency department, imaging, a four-suite outpatient surgery center, endoscopy and laboratory ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/beaufort-memorial-gets-green-light-hospital-bluffton)). Beaufort Memorial places it at 10 Innovation Dr and reports the building topped off on December 4, 2025, with completion late in 2026 and first patients early in 2027 ([Foundation updates](https://www.bmhsc.org/beaufortmemorialfoundation/vision-project-updates); [topping-off announcement](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/topping-celebration-bluffton-community-hospital)). A Bluffton hospital has been contested ground: an earlier 2018 application, for a 20-bed hospital, was withdrawn on September 10, 2024, and the contested case then dismissed, ending a legal fight of more than six years with the then-Tenet-owned Hilton Head and Hardeeville hospitals and Candler Hospital ([Post and Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/beaufort-county/news/bluffton-hospital-beaufort-memorial-canceled-lawsuit/article_80beb40e-71fa-11ef-92b6-e33bf46a3665.html)). Beaufort Memorial and MUSC had announced a Bluffton "micro hospital" joint venture in January 2018 ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/bmh-and-musc-announce-plans-to-open-bluffton-micro-hospital)); whether that structure governs the current project is not established.

**What membership means.** Beaufort Memorial operates a single hospital, but the admitting arrangement for its clinics is not published. **Two portals**: the hospital portal runs on MEDITECH ([account request page](https://www.bmhsc.org/NewPortalAccountRequest)), while the physician practices use athenahealth, where the system notes that some messaging carries a fee ([patient portals](https://www.bmhsc.org/patients-and-visitors/for-patients/request-medical-records/patient-portals)). No facility-fee notice was located on the billing pages, which do name several groups that bill separately — imaging, pathology and anesthesia ([billing and insurance](https://www.bmhsc.org/patients-and-visitors/for-patients/billing-insurance)). Financial assistance offers up to a 100% discount on medically necessary care for patients meeting income criteria, elective services excluded; the plain-language summary dated May 2026 sets the income limit at less than 300% of the current-year federal poverty guidelines, with additional asset and insurance conditions ([financial assistance](https://www.bmhsc.org/patients-and-visitors/for-patients/billing-insurance/financial-assistance)). A 2026 machine-readable charge file is posted ([billing and insurance](https://www.bmhsc.org/patients-and-visitors/for-patients/billing-insurance)).

**Organization facts.** As a governmental entity it files no Form 990, so no revenue, net-asset, community-benefit or executive-compensation figure is published through that route; audited financial statements exist but were not reviewed here ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/576000094)). Its president and chief executive is E. Russell Baxley III ([Beaufort Memorial](https://www.bmhsc.org/news/beaufort-memorial-hospital-names-new-president-ceo)). No official enforcement record was located in this research.

## HCA Healthcare — Memorial Health

HCA Healthcare, Inc., One Park Plaza, Nashville, Tennessee, is a for-profit company traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker HCA ([Form 10-K for FY2025, filed February 10, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526044769/hca-20251231.htm)). Note that CMS's ownership field for the Savannah hospital still reads "Voluntary non-profit," a stale entry that does not reflect the 2018 sale; the SEC filing is the current record.

**Hospital.** *Memorial Health University Medical Center*, 4700 Waters Ave, Savannah (CCN 110036), enrolled with Medicare under the legal name **Savannah Health Services, LLC** ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)). The hospital describes itself as an American College of Surgeons–verified Level I trauma center ([Memorial Health](https://www.memorialhealth.com/locations/memorial-health-university-medical-center); [About Us](https://www.memorialhealth.com/locations/memorial-health-university-medical-center/about-us)) — the only Level I trauma center among the hospitals in this comparison, according to the hospitals' own pages. Licensed beds could not be verified: published figures range from 512 to 711, none from a primary record. Estimated drive time is about 40 to 50 minutes; the free-flow route is 42 minutes and 26.5 miles.

**How it got here.** Memorial Health, then a nonprofit, was sold to HCA in a transaction that closed February 1, 2018, reported by local press at about $710 million including debt retirement and a ten-year capital commitment ([WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/story/37402290/sale-of-memorial-health-to-hca-final/); [HCA South Atlantic Division](https://hcasouthatlantic.com/about/newsroom/detail.dot?id=f280af26-9907-4962-8482-2edf49f1d26f)). The price is a press figure, not a filed one.

**Nearby, and no longer nearby.** Memorial Health's Bluffton/Okatie adult primary-care practice **closed at the end of December 2025**, per WTOC quoting Memorial Health ([WTOC, December 22, 2025](https://www.wtoc.com/2025/12/22/memorial-health-close-primary-care-practice-bluffton/)). No current Memorial Health clinic near Sun City was verified. The system publishes a 24/7 Consult-A-Nurse line, (844) 706-8773 ([Memorial Health](https://www.memorialhealth.com/locations/memorial-health-university-medical-center)). Planned: freestanding emergency rooms in Pooler and Richmond Hill, Georgia, announced September 3, 2025, after the Georgia Supreme Court declined an appeal of the state's approval; no opening dates or addresses were published ([Memorial Health](https://www.memorialhealth.com/about-us/newsroom/memorial-health-building-freestanding-emergency-rooms-in-pooler-and-richmond-hill); [WTOC](https://www.wtoc.com/2025/09/03/memorial-health-gets-green-light-freestanding-ers-pooler-richmond-hill/)). Both are Georgia sites; no South Carolina Certificate of Need for HCA was located.

**What membership means.** Memorial Health's site publishes two portal names, and the pages retrieved do not say which one holds the records of a given practice. Its homepage and its "Epic and MyChart" page name **MyChart**, with the login at mychart.hca-south.com ([Memorial Health](https://www.memorialhealth.com/locations/memorial-health-university-medical-center/for-patients/mychart), re-fetched August 25, 2026). The hospital's own About Us page separately promotes **MyHealthONE**, which it describes as letting patients view health records — lab results, physician notes, imaging reports — and "share your health records with a physician or caregiver" ([Memorial Health](https://www.memorialhealth.com/locations/memorial-health-university-medical-center/about-us), re-fetched August 25, 2026). The MyChart page, titled "Epic and MyChart," describes Epic as the electronic medical record system behind that portal and says the record "can be shared by participating doctors, hospitals, and patients," naming none; the About Us page does not say what records system sits behind MyHealthONE. Financial assistance for non-elective care runs in bands: household income at 0–200% of the federal poverty level qualifies for a 100% charity discount, and 201–400% for expanded assistance on balances above $1,500, with criteria that may vary by location and a separate uninsured discount ([financial assistance](https://www.memorialhealth.com/patient-resources/patient-financial-resources/financial-assistance)). A machine-readable charge file is posted ([pricing transparency](https://www.memorialhealth.com/patient-resources/patient-financial-resources/pricing-transparency-cms-required-file-of-standard-charges)). No facility-fee notice was located.

**Organization facts.** HCA reported total revenues of $75.600 billion and net income attributable to HCA of $6.784 billion for FY2025, operating 190 hospitals across 19 states and England ([Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526044769/hca-20251231.htm); [HCA fourth-quarter 2025 results](https://investor.hcahealthcare.com/news/news-details/2026/HCA-Healthcare-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-2025-Results-and-Provides-2026-Guidance/default.aspx)). Chief executive Samuel N. Hazen's total 2025 compensation was $26,456,606 ([proxy statement, filed March 13, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526106142/d939252ddef14a.htm)). Community benefit is not published — for-profit filers do not report it — and no Savannah-specific figures are separable from the corporate totals.

**Official records involving these names.** Three matters appear in official records, and the entity each one names matters.

- HCA's FY2025 Form 10-K discloses that in December 2023 the company was notified of an investigation by district attorneys in four **California** counties into waste-disposal practices at California facilities. The matter is described as open, and HCA states it is not expected to be material ([Form 10-K, Item 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860730/000119312526044769/hca-20251231.htm)).
- On December 23, 2015, **Memorial Health, Inc.; Memorial Health University Medical Center, Inc.; Provident Health Services, Inc.; and MPPG, Inc. d/b/a Memorial Health University Physicians** agreed to pay $9,895,043.04 to resolve *allegations* that they violated the False Claims Act by submitting claims to the government in violation of the Stark Law, in the words of the OIG page ([HHS Office of Inspector General](https://oig.hhs.gov/fraud/enforcement/government-settles-alleged-false-claims-act-violations-with-memorial-health-inc)). The page records allegations resolved by agreement; it states no admission or finding of liability, and it does not state the length of any corporate integrity agreement.
- On October 11, 2017, **Memorial Health, Inc.** of Savannah agreed to pay $264,848.36 to resolve *allegations* that it employed an individual it knew or should have known was excluded from participation in federal health care programs. The OIG page states the matter followed Memorial's own disclosure of the conduct to OIG pursuant to its corporate integrity agreement, and lists the enforcement type as "CIA Reportable Events" ([HHS Office of Inspector General](https://oig.hhs.gov/fraud/enforcement/memorial-health-agreed-to-pay-264000-for-allegedly-violating-the-civil-monetary-penalties-law-by-employing-an-excluded-individual/)).

Both OIG pages were re-fetched on the live oig.hhs.gov site on August 25, 2026 and support the dates, amounts and entity names above. Both matters predate HCA's February 2018 acquisition and name the **predecessor nonprofit entities**, not Savannah Health Services, LLC, the entity that bills today. No official action specific to Savannah Health Services, LLC was located in this research — which is not a finding that none exists.

### About the quality measures

The CMS figures above are **as published by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services** in the Care Compare hospital datasets released **August 13, 2026** (data modified July 22, 2026): the overall star rating from Hospital General Information ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/xubh-q36u/0)); patient experience from the HCAHPS survey covering **October 1, 2024 through September 30, 2025** ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/dgck-syfz/0)); the emergency-department median wait (measure OP-18b, discharged patients) for the same period ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/yv7e-xc69/0)); and the hospital-wide readmission measure for **July 1, 2024 through June 30, 2025** ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/632h-zaca/0)).

**HCAHPS** is CMS's standardized patient-experience survey, sent to a sample of discharged patients. The samples here are small and the response rates modest — 469 to 1,545 surveys per hospital, response rates of 16% to 29% ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/dgck-syfz/0)) — worth remembering before reading much into a few percentage points. CMS's own caveat applies to the star rating: it summarizes a selected set of measures, and hospitals are not measured on identical sets.

On readmissions, the hospital-wide measure is marked **"Not Available," CMS footnote 4 — data suppressed for one or more quarters — for all six hospitals**. That is a reporting status, not a deficiency, and this guide does not speculate about the reason. Condition-specific 30-day readmission rates (heart failure, pneumonia, COPD, heart attack; July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2025) *were* published for these hospitals, and every published rate is in CMS's "No Different Than the National Rate" category; the heart-attack measure is marked "number of cases too small" for Coastal Carolina and Candler ([CMS](https://data.cms.gov/provider-data/api/1/datastore/query/632h-zaca/0)).

**Leapfrog** is a private nonprofit that issues letter grades twice a year from a voluntary hospital survey plus public data. "Grade Not Available" means Leapfrog published no grade for that cycle; it is not a failing grade, and no explicit reason for Candler's is given on Leapfrog's page, which does carry a footnote that results are based on limited data due to a reported cybersecurity event, natural disaster, or CMS extraordinary circumstance. St. Joseph's Hospital's Spring 2026 grade could not be retrieved here; Leapfrog's separate ratings site shows a survey submission dated June 24, 2026 with no letter grade displayed ([Leapfrog ratings site](https://ratings.leapfroggroup.org/facility/details/11-0043/st-joseph-s-hospital-savannah-ga)).

**Accreditation is not verified anywhere in this guide.** The Joint Commission's public lookup could not be retrieved, so every accreditation statement above is the hospital's own.

## Questions worth asking

These are the questions this research could not answer for you, because the answers are not published. They are short enough to ask at a front desk.

1. **Which hospital does this practice admit to?** For Novant: Hilton Head or Coastal Carolina? For St. Joseph's/Candler: St. Joseph's or Candler? And does the answer change if I arrive by ambulance?
2. **Is this office owned by the health system, or independent?** It determines the portal, the financial-assistance policy, and whether a facility fee is possible.
3. **Is this location a hospital outpatient department that bills a separate facility fee — will I get two bills?** Ask whether the practice has told you in writing; do not assume a notice is required.
4. **Does my Medicare Advantage or commercial plan list this hospital *and* this clinic's billing entity as in-network?** Name the entity when you call — Hilton Head Medical Center, LLC, or Savannah Health Services, LLC, for instance — and write down the date you checked.
5. **If I go to a freestanding emergency department** — Tidewatch, Novant's Parkside Drive site, or Beaufort Memorial's coming Hilton Head location — **whose license and billing apply, and which hospital would I be transferred to if admitted?**
6. **When the two Bluffton hospitals open** — Beaufort Memorial late 2026 or early 2027, Novant in 2028 — **will my admitting hospital change, and which one does my practice plan to use?**
7. **Which financial-assistance policy applies to me, and what are its income thresholds?** For Novant, ask for the South Carolina policy, not the North Carolina one — the South Carolina document sets 300% of the federal poverty guidelines plus service-area, coverage and asset conditions, and asking confirms which conditions apply to you. For Beaufort Memorial, ask whether your physician is on the covered-provider list. For Memorial Health, ask which poverty-level band you fall in. For St. Joseph's/Candler, ask for the written policy.
8. **Which portal holds my records** — Beaufort Memorial has two separate logins, and Memorial Health's site names two portal brands, MyChart and MyHealthONE — **and can a doctor in another system see them?**
9. **Where does my doctor want me to go, for what kind of emergency, and would EMS take me somewhere else anyway?** For major trauma, the only hospital in this comparison whose own pages state Level I trauma-center status is Memorial Health in Savannah, about 42 minutes away by free-flow estimate.
10. **What does a lower CMS star or Leapfrog grade actually measure, and does it cover the service I would use?**
11. **Is the hospital-wide readmission measure still "not available" for my hospital, or has CMS published it since?**
12. **For the Savannah hospitals: how do out-of-state rules and ambulance transfers from South Carolina affect me?**

Coastal Carolina's count is in genuine conflict (44 on Novant's page, 37–41 in directories), and Memorial Health University Medical Center's published figures range from 512 to 711 with none traceable to a primary record.
- **No accreditation status was verified with The Joint Commission**; its public lookup was inaccessible throughout.
- **St. Joseph's Hospital's Spring 2026 Leapfrog grade could not be retrieved**, and the reason for Candler's "Grade Not Available" is not published.
- **No signed state Certificate of Need decision document was obtained for either approved Bluffton hospital.** For Novant's, South Carolina DPH's public-hearing notice was retrieved and supplies the project's square footage, cost and bed composition, but the source of the 33 transferred beds is not stated in it and the approval date and 2028 opening remain the system's own statements. For Beaufort Memorial's Bluffton Community Hospital, no state CON record was retrieved at all; its approval date and project details come from the system's announcement and press coverage.
- **St. Joseph's/Candler's financial-assistance income thresholds are not stated on its page.** Novant's South Carolina policy PDF, unreadable earlier in this research, was read for this merged version; its 300% threshold carries service-area, coverage and asset conditions that are summarized, not reproduced in full, above. Beaufort Memorial's own published figures conflict — less than 300% in the May 2026 plain-language summary used here, 320% in another summary PDF still posted — so ask which document controls.
- **Drive times to Novant's two Bluffton freestanding emergency departments were not computed** with a routing tool, and neither were the drive times used to exclude Colleton Medical Center and Hampton Regional.
- **Form 990 figures were updated at fact-check.** ProPublica displayed fiscal 2024 for Novant and fiscal 2025 for the three St. Joseph's/Candler entities when rechecked on August 24, 2026, later than the years two of the three research reports read; the figures here carry those years. Novant's reported net assets fell from $3.72 billion (2023) to $863 million (2024) in the extracts — a change this research did not examine.
- **Whether Novant's two South Carolina hospitals are fully on the MyNovant portal** was not separately verified, and neither was whether Paul P. Hinchey remains St. Joseph's/Candler's chief executive.
- **Beaufort Memorial's most recent audited financial statements were not reviewed**, so no revenue, net-asset or executive-compensation figure is reported for it.
- **Memorial Health publishes two portal names** — MyChart, with the login at mychart.hca-south.com, and MyHealthONE on the hospital's About Us page. Which applies to a given practice is not published; the MyChart page describes Epic as its records system, while the About Us page does not say what system is behind MyHealthONE.
- **The length of the corporate integrity agreement in the 2015 Memorial Health settlement is not stated on either OIG page.** A five-year term was reported earlier in this research from a Justice Department page that could not be opened, so it is not stated here. Both OIG pages were re-fetched live on August 25, 2026 and support the dates, amounts and entity names given.
- **Tidewatch's current operating status was not established** from a license or a current operating record. It appears on a Novant location page and in Novant's 2024 acquisition release; whether it operates alongside the Parkside Drive emergency department today, and under whose license each bills, is unverified.
- **Medicare Advantage network participation was not researched at all**, by design.
- Finally: for three of the four systems, no official enforcement record was located. That is a statement about what this search found. It is not a clean bill of health.
